Special Issue: Human–AI Collaboration and Sustainable HRM in the Asia–Pacific
Asia Pacific Journal of Human Resources

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "The verification paradox: Artificial intelligence, expertise erosion and sustainable human resource management", for consideration in the special issue on Human–AI Collaboration and Sustainable HRM in the Asia–Pacific.

The paper argues that generative artificial intelligence makes the ability to verify its outputs the scarce human contribution in knowledge work, while eroding the entry-level work through which that ability has been formed. Using a stock-and-flow view of capability and sustainable HRM, it develops propositions predicting that unchecked output accumulates as hidden verification debt, that organisations gaining most from entry-level substitution in the short run become most exposed later, and that individually rational cuts to junior hiring can produce a collective shortage of experienced staff. It derives contrasting propositions for offshore-service, internal labour market and market-based employment systems, and highlights the risk to Asia–Pacific economies that have relied on entry-level offshore services as a ladder into skilled work. Exploratory analyses of public labour force data show that young workers' employment has fallen in generative-AI-exposed occupations in the United States and Australia since 2022, but not clearly in Japan, consistent with the proposed differences between employment systems.

The content of this submission has not been published or submitted for publication elsewhere. All authors have contributed significantly to the manuscript and are in agreement with its content.
